# Business Case — Prior-Authorization Copilot

> Scope note: this is a decision-support demo built entirely on synthetic data. It does
> not connect to any real payer, EHR, or member record, and it never issues a binding
> coverage determination. See `docs/PROBLEM_STATEMENT.md` for the authoritative capstone
> requirements.

## Problem

Prior authorization (PA) is slow and repetitive. A payer receives a request from a
provider, then a human reviewer spends time doing the same three lookups on almost every
case: is the service covered under this member's benefit, does the clinical picture meet
the plan's medical-necessity criteria, and what specific criterion supports the outcome.
Meanwhile the provider and patient wait days for a decision that is often routine.

A copilot can compress the routine part of that work. It captures the incoming request,
checks the benefit, evaluates the medical-necessity criteria, and drafts a recommendation
with the criteria it relied on cited inline. The human reviewer stays the final
authority; the copilot just removes the mechanical lookups and gives the reviewer a
structured starting point. Clear-cut cases arrive nearly finished; ambiguous ones arrive
flagged, with the reason they could not be decided automatically.

## Actors

| Actor | Role in the system | Trust |
|---|---|---|
| Submitting provider | Source of the request and the clinical narrative | Untrusted input — text may be malformed, incomplete, or adversarial (prompt injection) |
| The copilot (this system) | Captures, checks benefits, evaluates criteria, drafts a recommendation | Assistive only — never the final decision-maker |
| Human clinical reviewer | Reviews the draft and issues the actual determination | Final authority |
| Payer benefit & criteria systems | Source of coverage rules and medical-necessity criteria | Modeled by the local MCP server over synthetic corpora |
| Member | Subject of the request | Synthetic; referenced only by a synthetic member id |

## Success metrics (synthetic demo)

1. **Every sample request produces a defensible output.** Each of the five sample
   requests yields a schema-valid `PADecision` that carries at least one cited criterion,
   or a `refer_clinical_review` disposition (with `criteria_status="indeterminate"` in the
   necessity assessment) to route for human review. No sample ever produces an unsupported
   determination.
2. **Clear-cut approvals are direct.** A request whose criteria are plainly met
   (`mri_lumbar_clearcut`) reaches an approve draft without a `human_review` detour.
3. **Uncertainty routes to a human.** Ambiguous criteria (`psg_indeterminate`), low model
   confidence, benefit gaps (`egd_not_covered`), missing required data
   (`knee_scope_missing_info`), an injected instruction
   (`injection_prompt_injection`), or a tool failure all route to `human_review` rather
   than a guessed determination.
4. **Cross-session memory changes behavior.** A determination stored during one session
   measurably changes the routing trace of a later session for the same member — for
   example, a prior referral or approval for a service is surfaced and shortens or
   redirects the later run.

## Scope guardrails

- **Decision-support only.** The system drafts and cites; it never issues a binding
  authorization.
- **Synthetic data only.** Benefits, providers, criteria, clinical guidance, and sample
  requests are all generated by `data/synthetic/generators.py`. No real payer or EHR
  connectivity.
- **Heuristics and stubs are acceptable.** The domain logic (benefit matching, criteria
  evaluation, necessity scoring) may be rule-based and approximate; the capstone value is
  the agentic orchestration, context engineering, memory, and MCP interoperability, not
  clinical accuracy.
- **Human in the loop by default.** `PADecision.human_review_required` defaults to `True`;
  the graph only clears it on an explicit clear-cut path.
