# Knee Arthroscopy with Meniscectomy - Medical Necessity

Arthroscopic partial meniscectomy helps patients whose knee mechanically locks, catches, or gives way because of a symptomatic meniscal tear. Multiple randomized trials show it does not reliably outperform structured physical therapy for degenerative meniscal change or osteoarthritis without true mechanical symptoms, so separating a mechanically unstable knee from painful arthritis is the decision that matters and the note should make that distinction explicit.

## Indications

- Mechanical symptoms (locking, catching, true giving way, or an inability to fully extend the knee) that persist despite conservative treatment.
- MRI or a positive provocative exam (for example a positive McMurray) consistent with a meniscal tear that correlates with the patient's symptoms.
- Effusion or joint-line tenderness localizing to the involved compartment.

## Step therapy

Expect a documented trial of physical therapy, activity modification, and analgesics or anti-inflammatories over roughly three months before surgery, with dates and the response recorded. A locked knee that cannot be straightened is the exception and may proceed without the full conservative course. Vague statements such as "failed conservative care" without dates route to the reviewer.

## Exclusions

- Isolated degenerative osteoarthritis or a degenerative tear without mechanical symptoms.
- No trial of conservative care in the prior three months and no locked knee.
- Advanced tricompartmental arthritis where arthroplasty is the appropriate step.
